📖 Rishab OS Guide

Welcome

Rishab OS is your personal command centre. It keeps your money, your recurring bills and investments, your client tasks, your knowledge notes, and your important documents together in one place, and saves everything privately to your own Google Drive, so nothing lives on anyone else's server. This guide walks you through each area in a few lines.

🏠

Home

Your daily snapshot. It shows tasks due today, bills and SIPs due soon, documents nearing expiry, your overall budget for the month, a Quick Scratchpad for stray thoughts, and Quick To-dos with tappable checkboxes.

💰

Financial dashboard

Every account lives here, grouped as Assets, holding Bank Accounts, Investments, and Other Assets, and Liabilities, holding Credit Cards and Other Liabilities. Add an account with the form at the top and choose its category. Each row has a three dots menu to rename it, edit its balance, set a group, view its transactions, or move it to another category.

Example. HDFC Flexicap and Nippon Small Cap, both labelled Mutual Funds, fold into a single Mutual Funds row showing their combined value, which you tap to see the split.
➕

Recording money

The plus button at the corner, or Record Transactions in the menu, opens the recorder. Choose Expense, Income, or Transfer. For an expense the Mode of Payment is the account you paid from, and for income it becomes Mode of Receipt. A Transfer moves money between your own accounts and is never treated as income or expense.

Paying a credit card bill is a Transfer from your bank to the card. It settles the card without counting the spend twice.
🔄

Recurring tracker

Anything that repeats sits here, rent, subscriptions, a monthly SIP, or a credit card bill. Tick an item as paid and it posts to your ledger and creates next month's copy on its own. For an Investments type you pick which holding it feeds through Invest into, so your investment grows as your bank falls.

✅

Task tracker

A flexible sheet for your client work. Drag a column edge to resize, use the three dots on any header to rename, sort, or delete a custom column, and add your own columns. Every task has an open icon that opens it as a full page with all its details, all editable, and a spacious Other Notes area for a long write up with checkboxes, images, and links.

📊

Cash flow analytics

This summarises where your money went for the month you pick. Set an overall budget and per category limits through Manage. The overall budget is a firm ceiling, so an expense that would cross it is stopped, with an option to raise it there. A category overspend is allowed but warns you, and draws from your Reserve Fund, a cushion you set for exactly this. You can export any month to CSV, and open any account to see its own transactions by month.

📚

Knowledge Base

Your notes, kept in folders. Create a folder, then a note, and write freely. The toolbar adds a checkbox item, an image, or a link, and pressing Enter after a checkbox makes the next one. Links open on a tap. You can also turn note checkboxes into tracker tasks with Sync To-Dos.

📁

Documents vault

Your important papers, a PAN, a registration, a certificate, each with a category, a Drive link, and an expiry date. Anything nearing expiry surfaces on your Home screen. Each document has a three dots menu to edit its details or remove it.

🔒

Privacy and security

Set a six digit PIN under Settings to lock the workspace, and add a fingerprint on a supported device for a quick unlock. The eye icon at the foot of the menu turns on Privacy Mode, which hides every figure across the app and stays on across a refresh. Your income can be kept hidden until you choose to reveal it.

☁️

How it saves

Everything saves to a single private file in your Google Drive, inside a folder named Rishab OS. Discrete actions save at once, and typing saves a moment later. If the connection ever pauses, it retries on its own and reconnects on your next tap.

A good first step

Add your accounts under Finance, record a few transactions, set a budget under Analytics, and add your first client task. From there the Home screen begins to fill with what matters today.
